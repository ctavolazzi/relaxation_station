## Last: the jungle river

The host set the cockpit aside ("this isn't working") and asked for a three.js scene: a boat going down a
jungle river endlessly, looping so it does not look like a loop. Published as "Jungle River"
(https://claude.ai/artifact/MVTqUyMqSBHzB1yEWGyGpp).

- It does not loop at all. The river's bends are sums of sines with unrelated periods, the banks wander on
  their own noise, and each 40 m stretch is replanted from its own seed when the boat leaves it behind.
  Each visit starts somewhere new. Waterfalls, ruins, fallen logs and herons turn up at irregular spots.
- Built on three.js 0.180 (the version johnnyautoseed pins) and its own addons: Sky, an adapted Water,
  bloom, SimplexNoise. The lifecycle code follows johnnyautoseed's `trainingStack.js`; the overlay uses the
  Warm Coffee palette with Fraunces and Nunito.
- A day passes in 40 minutes by default (or 8, or hold the hour). Five views: drift, bow, banks, canopy,
  auto. Sound is generated in the browser. "Hide controls" leaves a clean frame for screen recording.
- Tested in headless Chromium with software rendering: four views at four hours, 20 of 20 control checks,
  a re-centring of the world at 8,192 m with no gap, phone width with no sideways scroll. Frame rate on a
  real GPU was not measurable here; the page lowers its own detail if frames run slow.

## Then: two rounds of fixes from the host's phone

The host opened it on an iPhone and sent screenshots twice. The second time: "Well idk what control tests
you're running to make it "good" but they're not working exactly as planned. It's "ok" but there's still
many, many issues". They were right. The checks proved the buttons worked and the camera stayed over the
water; none of them looked at the picture. From then on every change was judged by rendering the views at
phone resolution (1170 by 2532), reading the images, and counting draw calls and triangles per frame.

Round one (v2):

- Coloured speckles on the iPhone. Phones now draw straight to the screen (no half-float buffer, no bloom),
  plants use a cheaper material, and leaf cards cast no shadows. The speckles never appeared here, so this
  is a mitigation, not a confirmed fix.
- The sunset turned grey and distant trees became white ghosts: a sky of our own, matched to the fog,
  takes over as the sun goes down. Stars no longer reflect in the water as streaks.
- Slow on the phone: 191 draw calls and 1.72 million triangles a frame became about 110 and 0.9 million
  (plants packed into fewer buffers, simpler bushes, the boat merged into one mesh, fewer stretches drawn).
- A log lying across the boat's path (now along the bank; 0 hits in 40,000 placements), a vine across the
  frame, ripple rings, a paper-thin roof (now thick thatch), the bank camera hidden in bushes.

Round two (v3):

- Stepped bands across calm water: the water's normal map used about ten of its 256 levels. It is now stored
  four times steeper with a little dither, and the shader scales it back.
- Blocky patches on the water: a square 256 px reflection stretched over a tall screen, and a shadow map
  spread over 140 m (14 cm a texel). The reflection now has the screen's shape; the shadow box is 80 m,
  centred ahead of the camera and moved in whole texels so edges do not crawl.
- Crowns like rocks: foliage outlines are now leaf cards around a smaller solid core, with a denser leaf
  texture. The big leaning tree over the water got the most of this.
- The river ran out to a bare water horizon: two rings of hazy far tree line just past the last planted
  stretch, tinted from the fog so they vanish in mist.
- Golden hour was washed out and the banks looked like brown cliffs: palette changes.
- The boat: seats and floor poked through the hull, its inside was painted teal, and from the bow seat it
  read as a dark pyramid. Parts are now cut to the hull, the inside is wood, and it has rails, ribs and a
  small deck at each end.
- Then the host asked whether the roof and the other pieces actually connect. They did not: measured from
  the build numbers, the canopy posts stopped 17 cm under the roof and floated 14 to 21 cm above the hull,
  the lantern post floated 33 cm, the floor board 8 cm. Now the posts stand on the seats and run into the
  roof, the lantern post stands on the forward seat, the paddle lies across two seats, and the floor is a
  raised board above the waterline (the river is one flat plane, so a lower floor showed water inside the
  boat). `joints.mjs` measures all 14 joints; every one is closed.
